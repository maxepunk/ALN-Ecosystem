## 1. The verdict

The approach is the right one. Requirements first, then architects who have not seen the agent's reasoning, then design rounds ordered by how hard a choice is to undo, with today's flaws as tests. All four reviews say so. Version 10 works against that approach in three places, and each can be fixed before the owner answers. The Decisions page asks the owner to choose ways to build (the fallbacks in B, the window's length in C, the pack's form in D) from the agent's own cost estimates, before the panel has compared anything, and those answers would reach the panel as binding. The requirements the blind round reads hold the agent's design choices in a handful of places, three of them marked settled against the page's own open questions. And the list of six decisions has no home for how the engine drives the room, which is about four lines in ten of today's server, or for passes, the kit's stored data, and installing and releasing after the first swap. With those fixed, this is the approach most likely to give the owner a long-term optimal architecture.

What the owner is asked is not yet clearly defined. The cut from eighteen questions to ten was right and J is sound. But three questions rest on a wrong fact: the TV does not freeze when the show machine dies, it goes dark; today's pack already holds checked when-then rules; a trial copy never drives the real room. Two questions hide a consequence that changes the answer (A with B gives a night with no results; C's first option breaks the rollback the owner ratified). Two are not the owner's by the page's own tests: H's rule was ratified on 2026-09-04, and I asks what the owner's decision of 2026-11-02 left to the build plan. One thing is asked in three places (may a device other than the show machine run the engine). After the changes below the page holds nine questions with about fifteen answers, six readings to confirm with yes or no, and one thing the panel truly waits for, J.
