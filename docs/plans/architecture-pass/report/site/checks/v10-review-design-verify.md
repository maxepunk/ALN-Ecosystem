# Verify: the review of the design approach (`v10-review-design.md`)

Written 2026-11-09 for the agent running the pass. A working file, so it calls the owner "the owner". Read only; this file is the one thing written.

## Scope and method

I checked all 22 findings of `v10-review-design.md` (six high, ten medium, six low) and the loose remarks in its five tables. I read the published text (`v10text/decisions.md`, `design.md`, and the cited parts of `requirements.md` and `index.md`), the sources (`report/site/v9/decisions.json`, `design.json`, `front.json`, the two requirements files, `build_site.py`), `DECISIONS.md`, the panel's brief, `ROADMAP.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, the program document, the owner's comments of 2026-11-02, and the code under `/home/user/ALN-Ecosystem`. I started each finding from "not a problem" and kept it only where I found the problem myself. I checked each proposed text against the owner's rules for the site, and each change against the page's four tests and against the rule that a change to a decision of the owner's goes to the owner as a question.

## Result

All 22 problems are real. Every code line and document line the review cites is accurate (list under "Facts checked"). Its count that 11 of 43 targets and 5 of 28 cases drive no decision is exact; I recounted from the links in `design.md`.

Four changes stand as proposed (D-M3, D-M10, D-L2, D-L4). Eighteen need a different change. Inside those I refuse eight parts:

1. Retitling or adding to the six decisions without asking (D-H3 as "for you to refuse", D-H4's new title, D-H5's seventh decision, D-M2's new title). The six are listed by name in the owner's decision "Design top-down, hardest-to-reverse choices first" (`DECISIONS.md:742-751`). A change to that list is the owner's, on the same ground the page gives for question J. What a round's "What it decides" text covers is wording, and the agent may widen it.
2. D-H6's script that drops each case's "Goes through" line from the blind folder. The owner's decision gives the panel the cases "as the owner reviewed them" (`DECISIONS.md:788-790`), and a script cannot judge "names only the pack". Fix the few lines at the source, so the owner reviews what the panel reads.
3. D-H2's status text naming "two installed versions on one machine". That puts a design option of the reviewer's into a requirement, which is the fault D-H6 finds elsewhere.
4. D-M4's exception written straight into the target and the lens. It is a reading of the owner's words ("if at all possible") that no earlier decision settles, so it goes under the readings to confirm.
5. D-M7's fourth architect. The owner's decision says "Three architects with different lenses" (`DECISIONS.md:797`). Cost does not make that the agent's call.
6. D-M8's "about a hundred devices" and its values taken from today's time-outs. "A few dozen devices" is in the owner's decision on the panel's setting (`DECISIONS.md:792`). Five seconds is when today's GM app gives up on a command (`ALNScanner/src/admin/utils/CommandSender.js:29`), not a target for a scan's answer.
7. D-L5's rule that drops sentences starting with "Today". It would miss most of them: today's facts sit in `why` (7 targets and cases), `numberToSet` (4), `case` (4), `status`, `when` and `throughWhat`, often mid-sentence.
8. D-L6's change to the Design page. That sentence already begins "For example".

Severity: I agree with the grades except two. D-H4 is medium: the protocol round and the authority round already list the pass targets as drivers; what is missing is the life of a pass. D-H1 is high for B, medium for D, and medium for C, whose three options are promises to adopters and so partly the owner's.

Two things the review's own changes need and it did not say: the builder accepts only two recommendation tags and hard-codes "The options" (V-3), and deferring B's mechanism leaves three other texts saying "the fallback you choose" (V-5).

## Final texts

**H1-B.** `decisions.json` `groups[0].questions[1].recommendation`, tag "What we need from you":

> From you now, three things, a line each. How long a game may go with no scans taken when the show machine dies for the night: not at all, a minute or two, or several minutes with a step the GM does by hand. What the players may lose for the rest of the night (video, music, lights, the TV) and what they must never lose. Whether the TV may keep counting while the engine is silent (yes or no). One reading of your words, on a kit with no second machine, is under the readings to confirm below. How the fallback is built comes back to you in the design round on how the engine holds the game's state, with what each way costs, from the panel. The four ways above are the ones the panel compares. Until then “Standalone games: declared, never mixed” stands as it is. If a design needs it amended, that comes to you as a question first.

**H1-C.** `groups[0].questions[2].recommendation`, tag changed to "What we need from you":

> From you now, two things. When a hardware scanner that missed its update meets a newer engine on a show night: must it work that night, with a warning on the GM's tablet, or may the engine refuse it? And do you want to promise other productions that a device or a pack made for one version keeps working with later versions for a stated time? You leaned to everything updating together (“Generally speaking, yes”); these two answers say whether that has an exception. The form of the version rules, and how long any window is, come back to you in the design round on the protocol's form, with what each costs. The agent's own view is the middle way above, for devices updated by cable. It is a view, not a finding.

**H1-D.** `groups[0].questions[3].recommendation`, tag changed to "What we need from you":

> From you now, two things. Must a game's maker be able to combine the engine's triggers and responses in ways no developer planned for, with no developer? And must such a combination also run in a standalone game, on a scanner alone? Whether that takes tables, when-then rules or something else comes back to you in the design round on how games and venues extend the engine, with what each costs to keep for years. Scripts stay out unless you say otherwise, because they end the standing rule that games are built without code.

**H1-I.** `decisions.json` `intro[1]`, added after the four tests:

> Where a question's options are ways to build something, you are asked only for what must be true at a show. The panel says what each way costs, and you choose among them in the design round.

**H2-S.** QT42 `situation` (title: "A release can be undone before the next game"):

> Nothing reaches the show machine until the owner says the new engine is ready. After that the engine changes only between events, while packs may be published at any time. If a new version fails on a show night, the owner puts the kit back on the version before it within a set time, with no developer and no terminal. The engine, the tablet and phone apps, the pack, the media and the venue profile go back together, and the check names any piece that did not. The records of nights already played on the new version are still on the kit. What a hardware scanner already updated for the new version does after the kit goes back is part of the owner's question “Release day: what must update together, and how long older devices work”. For the first swap the method is ratified: the owner replugs the old machine, which takes the same network address and name.

QT42 `numberToSet`: "how long going back may take: an engine requirement, stated for each supported machine".

**H2-T.** QT42 `status`, kind "partly settled":

**H2-E.** EX17 `case` (title: "A new engine version reaches the kit and can be taken back"):

> A new engine version is ready.
