Recommendations the candidate shape changed

Earlier question E

Question I: Keep the venue promise whole?

Before

Split the venue promise, then "Run wherever the venue and the game need", into "fit the game to the venue" and "run in any setup": fitting a game to a room is the venue technician's work (the profile, binding the pack's names to real equipment, the one preflight); running in any setup is standalone games, other kits and the open-source release. Recommendation: split, because the venue technician is one of the engine's five users and had no job of their own. Version 4 added the alternative of keeping one job renamed "Fit the game to the venue, whatever the setup".

After

One promise, titled The game fits the venue it is in tonight, with standalone games kept in its first sentences. Two titles fit the candidate shape: "The game fits the venue, whatever the setup" keeps standalone games and other kits in the title; "The game fits the venue it is in tonight" names the venue technician's moment and leaves them to the sentences. The cost of not splitting is that the GM running a standalone game and a future operator with another kit are named in a sentence, not in a title.

Why The candidate shape found that "run in any setup" has no part of its own. Fitting the game to tonight's venue is one part with an output, Venue fit: the venue technician's profile, which says what the kit brings, binds each of the pack's names to a real instrument and says whether the engine starts each program; Verdict turns it into one go or no-go. Running with no engine is a switch every design choice must allow for, declared as part of fitting the game. Running on another kit is each running copy of the engine keeping its own files, network ports and settings, Engine instance, the same part that keeps a test from touching the live show. A separate promise would own nothing.

Earlier question F

Question J: Proof and release in one promise?

Before

The change promise, then "Stay trustworthy as it changes", holds four kinds of work (tests that cannot hurt the live show, tests that follow the real path from a tap to a screen, checks that parts that pass things to each other agree, checks that what is installed matches). Split it into "Prove it works, away from the live show" and "Keep every part in step", and give each half an architecture pass question of its own. Version 4 added the option of widening one job's sentence to say a new version reaches the show only when the owner says it is ready, with rollback as plugging the old machine back in.

After

Keep one promise covering both moments, as its title does, and give the developer's tests and the release an architecture pass question each. The other options stay listed: split release day into a promise of its own; file release day with the venue technician's set-up work under The game fits the venue it is in tonight; or go back to the earlier wording, about tests alone, at the cost of leaving the owner's release day unnamed.

Why The candidate shape separates the promise's two moments, not its kinds of work. Before every change, the developer's tests pass and show the show still works for ALN and for the toy game; that is a quality every part must have, carried by Joins and proofs and Engine instance, and checking that parts that pass things to each other agree is part of it. On release day, the owner decides when a new version goes to the venue and can swap back by replugging a machine; that is one small part, Release, which checks that everything installed matches. The earlier split into "prove it works" and "keep every part in step" does not match the candidate shape, because keeping parts that pass things to each other in step is part of the testing. Both moments exist so that nothing reaches the live show unproven, or before the owner says so.

Earlier question G

Question K: Which promise carries equipment state on screens?

Before

Which job carries the equipment's state to every screen? The record job keeps the game's record true everywhere; the equipment job runs the equipment and knows its state; today the music or lights panel can stay wrong after a reconnect, and neither sentence says whose that is. Recommendation: the equipment job, because it keeps the game's record and the equipment's state apart and matches architecture pass question 2 (does a service's state travel with its health). A first draft of version 5 instead recommended the one-game promise, since the game's running state already includes the video queue, music, held items, the clock and each service's health, and would have moved Service health and service state travel on separate paths's card under it; it was withdrawn before version 5 was published.

After

Name the route that carries the equipment's state to every screen in neither promise, as long as the design rounds keep one delivery part; both then hold through it, and Service health and service state travel on separate paths's card stays under the room promise, where its moment at a show is felt. If the design rounds choose the other way, with the parts behind each promise delivering their own facts, the route belongs with the room promise, beside that card. If the owner still wants one promise to hold the words: under the room promise they sit with the equipment whose state it is, but that promise then names a route the other also needs; under the one-game promise, they match how the game's running state is already defined, but that promise then carries the equipment beside the game's record.

Why One part, Delivery, carries every fact to every screen, game facts and equipment alike, each with its label (how sure, how old). Equipment decides its own state and health as one dated fact; Delivery carries it; the screens draw it. The two promises already cover their halves: under the room promise the GM makes the room do a thing and sees it happen, or sees at once that it did not; under the one-game promise every screen agrees, also after a tablet comes back. So neither sentence needs to name the route.

The words

  • Promise, not job. A promise is what a person can count on at a given moment. The pass's word for the same list from 2026-10-31 was "job"; the owner found it forced once the jobs were restated from people's side and mapping how they fit together split them into promises, qualities and parts. The three earlier working documents that say "job" keep their words and carry a note giving each job's promise title.
  • Venue technician, not "installer" or "venue installer": the person who fits a kit to a venue, satisfies the pack's tech rider, fills in the venue profile and runs the preflight (decision 27).
  • Titles, not numbers. Promises carry their titles only, so a card reads without memorizing ids; the numbers appear nowhere a reader sees, and in the headings, the glance table, the cross-links and the diagrams' labels the titles replace them.
  • Parts keep promises; design flaws break them. "Kept by" and "break" are the page's words for that from version 6, and "broken by" from version 8; a part card says "Mainly keeps" and "Also needed by".
  • Quality: something every part must have so a promise holds everywhere, never a promise itself. The summary's line that two jobs were mostly qualities became two promises kept by a quality, because a quality is never a promise.
  • Moment: when a promise matters, a production stage (Author, Rehearse, Run, Review), a point inside a show, or release day.
  • Part: one of the 17 pieces of the candidate design, and only that. Before, "part" meant a piece of the engine at any size; what keeps a copy of the pack is now a place, today's code is modules or code, a kit's copies are copies, and module, interface and seam are defined without the word.
  • Decision 27 made the candidate shape's restated title a promise: "Fit the game to the venue it is in tonight" became The game fits the venue it is in tonight.
  • Shared work several promises depend on needs no word of its own: each piece becomes one part, so the candidate shape's "shared mechanisms" are folded into the part cards.
  • Design flaw, not cause, for C1 to C16 in owner-facing text, from version 3; the owner said "cause analysis" does not land. "Green run" is said as a passing test run from version 5.
  • The earlier candidate, from version 9, for the candidate shape of 17 parts, drawn bottom-up from today's engine in version 5 and shown on version 8's Parts page. It is one input among the panel's; the panel compares it with its own proposals in its second round. Its part Joins and proofs is replaced by the published protocol and the test bench (One published protocol and an outside test bench). "Candidate shape" stays in the earlier entries on this page.
  • Test situations, from version 9, for what the pages called test cases: the sixteen design flaws, which every design is checked against, and each promise's test cases. "Where today's engine falls short" replaces "Broken by" on the Requirements page, and "design choice" replaces "rule" everywhere, since on these pages a rule reads as a game rule.
