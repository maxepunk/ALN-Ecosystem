C1 Each tablet keeps its own list of claimed tokens

C2 Service health and service state travel on separate paths

  • Before, the card left the record and updates with the music service. Now Equipment keeps one dated record of each thing the room does and Delivery sends each change by one route, so every panel sees outages and recoveries.

C3 Engine trusts its stale picture of outside programs

  • Before, the card let the code that reads each program own the engine's picture of it. Now Equipment owns one dated picture per thing the room does; Show runner reads it and keeps no copy; Delivery carries every correction.

C4 Nothing says what a game boundary clears or keeps

  • Before, the card's table of moments had no owner. Now Lifecycle owns it, alone moves the game and calls every reset; Delivery tells every screen and player device each moment as a fact, so none has to guess.

C5 Boot and Reset System wire the engine differently

  • Before, the card proposed one routine for engine start and Reset System. Now a starting point builds every part once from one list, and Lifecycle runs both as one path, so music is never left out and nothing wired twice.

C6 Reconnect snapshot and live update are built apart

  • Before, the card had each engine part describe its facts. Now Delivery builds each fact once, for catch-up and live change, and a restore after a reload is sent like a change, so the History badge and clock wake.

C7 A reply never says which request, final or stored

  • Before, the card's reply part saved and answered. Now the Game record saves and Answering replies through one door for all devices, with one list of reply words, so a batch with no game gets one answer: stored or refused.

C8 Engine and tests both assume they own the machine

  • The card already gave each engine its own names. Now every test run and the designer's preview engine are engines too, built the same way under their own names, so a test or rehearsal never ends the show's video player.

C9 Tablets and phones decide facts the engine owns

  • Before, the card left the phone alone and had the standalone tablet load shared rules. Now one Game record judges every GM tap, on the engine or on a standalone tablet, and player devices draw the engine's reply, so a rule change lands once.

C10 Screens show a normal value when nothing is known

  • Before, the health list alone said whether a fact was known, unknown or stale. Now every fact Delivery sends carries that label, so the TV clock and music card show old or down as the health panel does.

C11 Several places answer the same question, each differently

C12 Pack edits reach each copy of the pack differently

C13 The engine does not know who is connected

  • Before, the card's role table both knew who was connected and sent facts. Now Connections and roles knows who each connection is, Delivery and Answering ask it, and every device, phones included, shows a pass, so three checks become one.

Recommendations the map changed

Question E

Before

Split the venue promise, then "Run wherever the venue and the game need", into "fit the game to the venue" and "run in any setup": fitting a game to a room is the venue technician's work (the profile, the bindings, the one preflight); running in any setup is standalone games, other kits and the open-source release. Recommendation: split, because the venue technician is one of the engine's five users and had no job of their own. Version 4 added the alternative of keeping one job renamed "Fit the game to the venue, whatever the setup".

After

One promise, titled The game fits the venue it is in tonight, with standalone games kept in its first sentences. Two titles fit the map: "The game fits the venue, whatever the setup" keeps standalone games and other kits in the title; "The game fits the venue it is in tonight" names the venue technician's moment and leaves them to the sentences. The cost of not splitting is that the GM running a standalone game and a future operator with another kit are named in a sentence, not in a title.

Why The map found that "run in any setup" has no part of its own. Fitting the game to tonight's venue is one part with an output, Venue fit: the venue technician's profile, which says what the kit brings, binds each of the pack's names to a real instrument and says whether the engine starts each program; Verdict turns it into one go or no-go. Running with no engine is a switch every rule takes, declared as part of fitting the game. Running on another kit is each engine knowing its own names, Engine instance, the same part that keeps a test from touching the live show. A separate promise would own nothing.

Question F

Before

The change promise, then "Stay trustworthy as it changes", holds four kinds of work (tests that cannot hurt the live show, tests that prove the real path, checks that joined parts agree, checks that what is installed matches). Split it into "Prove it works, away from the live show" and "Keep every part in step", and give each half a question of its own in the pass. Version 4 added the option of widening one job's sentence to say a new version reaches the show only when the owner says it is ready, with rollback as plugging the old machine back in.

After

One promise, as the title A change is proven before it reaches anyone, and the owner alone sends it to the show has it, covering both moments, and give the proof and the release a question each in the pass. The other options stay listed: split release day into a promise of its own; file release day with the venue technician's deploy work under The game fits the venue it is in tonight; or go back to the earlier wording, about tests alone, at the cost of leaving the owner's release day unnamed.

Why The map separates the promise's two moments, not its kinds of work. Before every change, the developer's tests pass and show the show still works for ALN and for the toy game; that is a quality every part must have, carried by Joins and proofs and Engine instance, and checking that joined parts agree is part of it. On release day, the owner decides when a new version goes to the venue and can swap back by replugging a machine; that is one small part, Release, which checks that everything installed matches. The earlier split into "prove it works" and "keep every part in step" does not match the map, because keeping joined parts in step is part of the proof. Both moments exist so that nothing reaches the live show unproven, or before the owner says so.

Question G

Before

Which job carries the equipment's state to every screen? The record job keeps the game's record true everywhere; the equipment job runs the equipment and knows its state; today the music or lights panel can stay wrong after a reconnect, and neither sentence says whose that is. Recommendation: the equipment job, because it keeps the game's record and the equipment's state apart and matches the pass's question 2 (does a service's state travel with its health). A first draft of version 5 instead recommended the record promise, since the game's running state already includes the video queue, music, held items, the clock and each service's health, and would have moved C2's card under it; the published version 5 withdrew that.

After

Add the route to neither promise, as long as step 3 keeps one delivery part; both then hold through it, and C2's card stays under The room does what the designer cued and the GM asked, where its moment at a show is felt. If step 3 chooses the other way, with the parts behind each promise delivering their own facts, the route belongs with the room promise, beside C2's card. If the owner still wants one promise to hold the words: under the room promise they sit with the equipment whose state it is, but that promise then names a route the other also needs; under One game, the same on every screen they match how the game's running state is already defined, but that promise then carries the equipment beside the game's record.

Why One part, Delivery, carries every fact to every screen, game facts and equipment alike, each with its label (how sure, how old). Equipment decides its own state and health as one dated fact; Delivery carries it; the screens draw it. The two promises already cover their halves: under the room promise the GM makes the room do a thing and sees it happen, or sees at once that it did not; under the record promise every screen agrees, also after a tablet comes back. So neither sentence needs to name the route.

The words

  • Promise, not job. A promise is what a person can count on at a given moment. The pass's word for the same list from 2026-10-31 was "job"; the owner found it forced once the jobs were restated from people's side and the map split them into promises, qualities and parts. The three dated records that say "job" keep their words and carry a note mapping each job to its promise title.
  • Venue technician, not "installer" or "venue installer": the person who fits a kit to a venue, satisfies the pack's tech rider, binds the installation profile and runs the preflight (decision 27).
  • Titles, not numbers. Promises carry their titles only, so a card reads without memorizing ids; the numbers appear nowhere a reader sees, and in the headings, the glance table, the cross-links and the diagrams' labels the titles replace them.
  • Parts keep promises; design flaws break them. "Kept by" and "break" are the page's words for that from version 6, and "broken by" from version 8; a part card says "Mainly keeps" and "Also needed by".
  • Quality: something every part must have so a promise holds everywhere, never a promise itself. The summary's line that two jobs were mostly qualities became two promises kept by a quality, because a quality is never a promise.
  • Moment: when a promise matters, a production stage (Author, Rehearse, Run, Review), a point inside a show, or release day.
  • Part: one of the 17 pieces of the candidate design, and only that. Before, "part" meant a piece of the engine at any size; a pack's holders are now places, today's code is modules or code, a kit's copies are copies, and module, interface and seam are defined without the word.
  • The seventh title becomes a promise: the map's restatement "Fit the game to the venue it is in tonight" became The game fits the venue it is in tonight.
  • Shared work several promises depend on needs no word of its own: each piece becomes one part, so the map's "shared mechanisms" are folded into the part cards.
  • Design flaw, not cause, for C1 to C16 in owner-facing text, from version 3; the owner said "cause analysis" does not land. "Green run" is said as a passing test run from version 5.
