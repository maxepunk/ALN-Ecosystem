# Standing rules, version 9: writer's log

Written 2026-11-01 for `site/v9/rules.json`, the standing rules of the
Requirements page (plan v2 section 3.3, item 5). Source: the roadmap,
`docs/plans/ROADMAP.md`, section 2, "Standing constraints
(owner-ratified, all blocks)", all six subsections. Wordings follow the
jargon table (`report/site/checks/jargon-table.md`); the rows used are
named below. The file was generated by a script kept in the scratchpad
so that the slugs follow the plan's rule by computation and the checks
(slug uniqueness, three to eight words per title, one or two sentences
per line, no em dashes, JSON parses) ran on the output; the script is
not part of the deliverable.

Count: 20 rules in 6 groups. The roadmap's six subsections map one to
one onto the groups. Two roadmap statements each yield two rules (noted
below); one roadmap checklist item is carried by a rule in another
group rather than repeated (noted below). No constraint was added or
strengthened.

## One line per rule

Format: group, title: the roadmap sentence(s) it comes from (roadmap
section 2 wording, quoted or close), then the wording changes made.

### Identity and player data

- Identity and player data, Players never have accounts: "Players never
  have accounts. Nothing player-facing ever gets a user-facing login,
  phones and scanners included (2026-08-29, reaffirming the one-auth
  principle)." "Player-facing" became "a player uses"; "user-facing
  login" became "asks a player to log in". The source renders "the
  one-auth principle" as "the engine's one design for who gets access"
  (jargon row 52 turns the access vocabulary into plain words; the
  principle itself has no table row).
- Identity and player data, The engine knows devices, not people: "The
  engine's only built-in identity is the device or session id (the
  existing deviceId system, extended to phone sessions in the
  players'-phones block). Person-level identity is game policy, never an
  engine default." The parenthetical about today's deviceId system is
  dropped from the line: today's engine is evidence, never the reason
  (plan v2 section 6), and the rule stands without it. "Person-level
  identity" became "naming a person", the wording the front page's
  words box already uses.
- Identity and player data, How players are identified is the game's
  choice: "A game's design may add identification moments through
  whatever its hardware supports: a typed name on a personal phone, a
  scanned object (a band, a character token) at a shared station, or
  nothing at all. 'Claim-credit' is one optional pattern for
  personal-device games, not a platform rule." This is the second half
  of the roadmap's rule 2, split into its own rule because a line holds
  at most two sentences and the two halves say different things (what
  the engine builds in; what the game may add). The background sentence
  on why a phone points at a person and a station at equipment is
  explanation, not a constraint, and is left out. "Claim-credit" became
  "letting a player claim credit".
- Identity and player data, Game data stays on the kit: "Game data stays
  on the kit unless the owner moves it." Unchanged.
- Identity and player data, The owner sets how long data is kept: "How
  long old session data is kept is the owner's decision, per game."
  "Session data" became "game data" (CONTEXT.md and the site say "game"
  for the night's play; "session" has several meanings).
- The closing sentence of the subsection, "These four rules are the
  privacy defaults that every future open-source operator inherits", is
  carried in the second intro paragraph, since the shape gives a group
  no text of its own.

### Growth without corners

- Growth without corners, Never assume one kit, one pack, one
  production: "No block may hard-code 'one kit, one pack, one
  production' assumptions where avoiding them costs little (owner,
  2026-08-29)." and "Multi-kit and multi-pack features belong to the
  operations era (§7.4). The seams that make them possible are built
  now." "Hard-code" became "build in". The qualifier "where avoiding
  them costs little" is kept. The operations era is named, not numbered.
- Growth without corners, Pack and venue profile chosen, never assumed:
  checklist items "the pack is selected by path or id, never assumed to
  be the only one; the profile likewise". The two items share one rule
  because the roadmap states the second only as "likewise". "Profile"
  became "the venue profile" (the site's name for it; jargon rows D6,
  P80).
- Growth without corners, No one kit's names inside engine code:
  checklist item "no kit-unique identifiers inside engine code".
  Rendered as the site already does in jargon row P81 ("no names unique
  to one kit in engine code").
- Growth without corners, Network names and addresses work per kit:
  checklist item "the DNS/IP model works per-kit". "DNS/IP" became "the
  way the kit's devices find each other by name and address" (jargon row
  69: one-off engineering words become a plain sentence; row P81 says
  "the kit's own network").
- The checklist item "media bundles can move between machines (§2.3)" is
  not repeated here. It is the media model's requirement (b) and is the
  rule "Media bundles move between machines" in the media group. One
  rule per constraint, so it appears once.

### The media model

- The media model, Media never lives inside the pack: "Media files never
  live in git packs (owner, 2026-08-29 ...). The pack names its media
  files, with hashes." and "Carrying files inside packs is out of the
  current era entirely (2026-08-29, Q10)." "Git packs" became "the
  pack" (the site never says git). "Hashes" became "a fingerprint of its
  contents" (jargon row 64). The question number Q10 is dropped; the
  date stays in the source. The roadmap's aside that this is already
  the repo's practice is left out: today's engine is evidence, never the
  reason.
- The media model, A managed media bundle carries the files: "A managed
  media bundle carries the actual files." and "(The hardware scanner's
  asset-sync channel already works this way and is the model.)"
  "Asset-sync channel" became "already gets its files this way" (jargon
  row 29 for the hardware scanner; row 69 for the engineering word).
  This is the second rule from the subsection's opening statement,
  split from the first because the two say different things (what the
  pack holds; what carries the files).
- The media model, A designer can upload the pack's media: "A designer
  uploads files through the design tool" and requirement "(a) a designer
  can work through and upload the files their pack needs". "The design
  tool" is the site's name (front.json and design.json use it). The
  scheduling note "the full upload experience is content-tooling work"
  is not a constraint and is left out of the line.
- The media model, Media bundles move between machines: "a bundle can be
  checked against the pack's list and moved between orchestrator
  machines for different game instances" and requirement "(b) bundles
  move easily between machines". "Orchestrator machines" became "engine
  machines" (jargon row 66). The scheduling note "reference-checking
  arrives with the pack-manager page" is not a constraint and is left
  out of the line.

### The design-iteration loop

- The design-iteration loop, Games are playtested without code or
  settings files: "Making games playtestable without code or config
  files IS the point of the engine work (owner, 2026-08-29). The loop:
  edit pack values in the tool, apply, playtest, repeat." "Config files"
  became "a settings file" (the front page's words box wording).
- The design-iteration loop, The editor and live apply ship together:
  "Consequences: the mechanics editor is the loop's surface, and
  hot-apply (reload a pack without restarting the orchestrator;
  historical: E10) is what makes the loop fast instead of
  restart-per-change. The ratified floor: the editor and hot-apply are
  one deliverable (§8.6)." "Hot-apply" became "applying a change while
  the engine runs, between games" (jargon row 16); the title's short
  form is "live apply", which the line spells out. "Loop's surface"
  became "where the loop happens". The historical id E10 and the
  registry number are dropped. "One deliverable" is kept in the line.

### Platform parity

- Platform parity, Phones and hardware scanners stay equal in ability:
  "Phone/web clients and the hardware scanners stay equal in engine
  capabilities: both can drive the same pack-declared interactions,
  including the interaction primitives (§7.1)." "Clients" became
  "devices" (jargon row 40); "interaction primitives" became "multi-tap
  player actions" (row 57); "engine capabilities" became "what the
  engine lets them do" (the front page's words box wording; row 20
  warns that "capability" collides with the planned capabilities).
  The source carries "(2026-08-29; reaffirms the 2026-07-18 'the
  hardware scanner is a first-class platform' ruling)".
- Platform parity, Equal in ability, not in screens: "Parity does NOT
  mean identical user interfaces: hardware differs (keyboard, screen
  size, personal vs shared), and a game's kit declares which device
  classes its players use and what part each plays." "User interfaces"
  became "screens"; "device classes" became "kinds of device". Split
  from the first parity rule because the roadmap's one paragraph holds
  four distinct statements and a line holds two sentences.
- Platform parity, Hardware scanner work waits on no game:
  "Hardware-scanner firmware work for this is committed platform
  engineering: it does not wait on any one game's design." "Firmware"
  became "the hardware scanner's code" (jargon row 69); "committed
  platform engineering" became "platform work the engine commits to".
- Platform parity, Designers change pack data, never device code:
  "Everything a designer iterates on is pack data, never firmware. The
  standing principle: never design a mechanic whose iteration loop
  requires re-flashing a fleet of devices." "Firmware" became "the code
  built into a device"; "re-flashing a fleet of devices" became
  "reloading the code on every hardware scanner" (jargon row 69).

### The engine holds no opinion

- The engine holds no opinion, The engine holds no opinion on game
  design: "Game-design choices (identification, rosters, station
  scarcity, tracking policy, whether more engagement is better) are pack
  and kit parameters, never engine defaults (reaffirmed 2026-08-29)."
  "Identification" became "how players are identified"; "station
  scarcity" became "how scarce stations are"; "tracking policy" became
  "what is tracked". The list is kept whole.

## Checks against how the site already names the rules

Checked against the words-box entry "The standing rules" in
`v9/front.json` (`words[5]`) and the rules `v9/design.json` cites
(`ahead[3].settled`: "the engine holds no opinion about game design,
media never lives inside the pack, and nothing in the engine assumes
one kit, one pack or one production").

1. "Nothing in the engine assumes one kit, one pack or one production"
   (design.json) and "nothing assumes one kit, one pack or one
   production" (front.json words box). The roadmap says "No block may
   hard-code 'one kit, one pack, one production' assumptions where
   avoiding them costs little." The site's short form drops the
   qualifier and so reads stronger than the roadmap. The rule follows
   the roadmap and keeps "where avoiding it costs little". Both site
   sentences are summaries, not rule text, so they are not changed by
   this file; a later consistency pass may want them to carry the
   qualifier or to point at the rule.
2. "Media never lives inside the pack" (both files). The roadmap says
   "Media files never live in git packs"; the pack names its files. The
   two agree; the rule's title uses the site's wording.
3. "The engine holds no opinion about game design" (both files). The
   roadmap's section 2.6 says the same. Agree.
4. Front.json's words box: "players never have accounts" (agree);
   "the engine's only built-in identity is the device or session id,
   and naming a person is game policy, never an engine default" (agree;
   the words box does not mention that a game may add its own
   identification moments, which the roadmap states and the rule "How
   players are identified is the game's choice" keeps); "game data
   stays on the kit, and how long it is kept is your decision, per
   game" (agree); "a game is playtested with no code or settings file,
   by changing a value in the tool, applying it and playing it" (agree;
   the words box does not mention that the editor and the live apply
   are one deliverable, which the rule "The editor and live apply ship
   together" keeps); "phones and hardware scanners stay equal in what
   the engine lets them do, not in their screens" (agree; the words box
   does not carry the two further parity statements, that the scanner
   work waits on no game and that designers change pack data, never
   device code, which the rules keep). These are omissions of a summary,
   not disagreements; the rules follow the roadmap's full text.
5. No site text was found that contradicts a roadmap constraint outright.
   The one divergence in strength is item 1.

  historical ids (E10) or registry rows (§8.1, §8.6) appear in the
  file. Sources name the roadmap section in plain words and the

  left out; the hardware scanner's asset sync stays only because the
