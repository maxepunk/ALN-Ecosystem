# Standing rules, version 9: writer's log

Written 2026-11-01 for `site/v9/rules.json`, the standing rules of the
Requirements page (plan v2 section 3.3, item 5). Source: the roadmap,
`docs/plans/ROADMAP.md`, section 2, "Standing constraints
(owner-ratified, all blocks)", all six subsections. Wordings follow the
jargon table (`report/site/checks/jargon-table.md`); the rows used are
named below. The file was generated by a script kept in the scratchpad
so that the slugs follow the plan's rule by computation and the checks
(slug uniqueness, three to eight words per title, one or two sentences
per line, no em dashes, JSON parses) ran on the output; the script is
not part of the deliverable. Revised the same day after the check
`checks/v9-check-rules.md`; the last section records each of its rows.

Count: 20 rules in 6 groups. The roadmap's six subsections map one to
one onto the groups. Two roadmap statements each yield two rules (noted
below); one roadmap checklist item is carried by a rule in another
group rather than repeated (noted below). Every rule keeps the
roadmap's qualifiers and scope; the check found three places where the
first draft had strengthened or narrowed one, and they are corrected.

## One line per rule

Format: group, title: the roadmap sentence(s) it comes from (roadmap
section 2 wording, quoted or close), then the wording changes made.

### Identity and player data

- Identity and player data, Players never have accounts: "Players never
  have accounts. Nothing player-facing ever gets a user-facing login,
  phones and scanners included (2026-08-29, reaffirming the one-auth
  principle)." "Player-facing" became "a player uses"; "user-facing
  login" became "shows a login"; "phones and scanners included" is kept
  as the roadmap has it, so the two are examples, not a closed list. The
  source renders "the one-auth principle" as "the engine's one design
  for who gets access" (jargon row 52 turns the access vocabulary into
  plain words; the principle itself has no table row).
- Identity and player data, The engine knows devices, not people: "The
  engine's only built-in identity is the device or session id (the
  existing deviceId system, extended to phone sessions in the
  players'-phones block). Person-level identity is game policy, never an
  engine default." The parenthetical about today's deviceId system is
  dropped from the line: today's engine is evidence, never the reason
  (plan v2 section 6), and the rule stands without it. "Session id"
  here means a player phone's session (CONTEXT.md "session", meaning 3,
  the session tier: player phones, pseudonymous), not the game's id
  number that jargon row 50 gives for "session id"; the line spells it
  out as "on a player's phone, the id that phone is given for the
  night". "Person-level identity" became "knowing which person is
  which": a scanned band identifies a person with no name, as the next
  rule says, so "naming" would narrow it.
- Identity and player data, How players are identified is the game's
  choice: "A game's design may add identification moments through
  whatever its hardware supports: a typed name on a personal phone, a
  scanned object (a band, a character token) at a shared station, or
  nothing at all. 'Claim-credit' is one optional pattern for
  personal-device games, not a platform rule." This is the second half
  of the roadmap's rule on built-in identity, split into its own rule
  because a line holds at most two sentences and the two halves say
  different things (what the engine builds in; what the game may add).
  The background sentence on why a phone points at a person and a
  station at equipment is explanation, not a constraint, and is left
  out. "Claim-credit" became "letting a player claim credit".
- Identity and player data, Game data stays on the kit: "Game data stays
  on the kit unless the owner moves it." Unchanged.
- Identity and player data, The owner sets how long data is kept: "How
  long old session data is kept is the owner's decision, per game."
  "Session data" became "game data" (CONTEXT.md and the site say "game"
  for the night's play; "session" has several meanings).
- The closing sentence of the subsection, "These four rules are the
  privacy defaults that every future open-source operator inherits", is
  carried in the second intro paragraph, since the shape gives a group
  no text of its own. "Operator" became "everyone who runs the
  open-source engine in future": jargon row 52 gives "operator" the
  sense "staff who log in, such as the GM", which is not the roadmap's
  sense here.
- The sources name each roadmap rule by what it says (the rule on
  accounts, the rule on built-in identity, the rule on where game data
  stays, the rule on keeping old data), not by its list position:
  titles, not numbers (plan v2 section 2), and no other group cites a
  position.

### Growth without corners

- Growth without corners, Avoid one-kit assumptions where avoiding them
  is cheap: "No block may hard-code 'one kit, one pack, one production'
  assumptions where avoiding them costs little (owner, 2026-08-29)." and
  "Multi-kit and multi-pack features belong to the operations era
  (§7.4). The seams that make them possible are built now." "Hard-code"
  became "build in". The qualifier "where avoiding them costs little" is
  kept in the line and carried into the title, so the title reads no
  stronger than the roadmap. The second sentence keeps the roadmap's
  order and scope: features for several kits or several packs belong to
  the operations era; the seams that make them possible are built now.
  The operations era is named, not numbered.
- Growth without corners, Pack and venue profile chosen, never assumed:
  checklist items "the pack is selected by path or id, never assumed to
  be the only one; the profile likewise". The two items share one rule
  because the roadmap states the second only as "likewise"; the line
  spells "likewise" out in full for the profile (chosen the same way,
  and never assumed to be the only one either). "Profile" became "the
  venue profile" (the site's name for it; jargon rows D6, P80).
- Growth without corners, No one kit's names inside engine code:
  checklist item "no kit-unique identifiers inside engine code".
  Rendered as the site already does in jargon row P81 ("no names unique
  to one kit in engine code").
- Growth without corners, Network names and addresses work per kit:
  checklist item "the DNS/IP model works per-kit". "DNS/IP" became "the
  way the kit's devices find each other by name and address" (jargon row
  69: one-off engineering words become a plain sentence; row P81 says
  "the kit's own network").
- The checklist item "media bundles can move between machines (§2.3)" is
  not repeated here. It is the media model's requirement on moving
  bundles and is the rule "Media bundles move between machines" in the
  media group. One rule per constraint, so it appears once.

### The media model

- The media model, Media never lives inside the pack: "Media files never
  live in git packs (owner, 2026-08-29 ...). The pack names its media
  files, with hashes." and "Carrying files inside packs is out of the
  current era entirely (2026-08-29, Q10)." "Git packs" became "the
  pack" (the site never says git). "Hashes" became "a fingerprint of its
  contents" (jargon row 64). The question number Q10 is dropped; the
  date stays in the source. The roadmap's aside that this is already
  the repo's practice is left out: today's engine is evidence, never the
  reason.
- The media model, A managed media bundle carries the files: "A managed
  media bundle carries the actual files." This is the second rule from
  the subsection's opening statement, split from the first because the
  two say different things (what the pack holds; what carries the
  files). The roadmap's bracketed aside "(The hardware scanner's
  asset-sync channel already works this way and is the model.)" is left
  out of the rule: it is an aside about today's engine, not one of the
  subsection's constraints, and the panel gets the standing rules before
  it may see today's engine (decision "What the architects' panel sees,
  and when"); the sentence belongs on Today's engine as evidence, if
  anywhere.
- The media model, A designer can upload the pack's media: "A designer
  uploads files through the design tool", requirement "(a) a designer
  can work through and upload the files their pack needs" of "Two
  requirements the final tool must meet", and "The full upload
  experience is content-tooling work (§7.3)". "The design tool" is the
  site's name (front.json and design.json use it). The line says "the
  design tool, when finished, must let", so the rule binds the finished
  tool, as the roadmap does, not every block now; the roadmap's timing
  is kept in the line as "the full upload experience comes with the
  content tooling block" (the block by name, as the site names it).
- The media model, Media bundles move between machines: "a bundle can be
  checked against the pack's list and moved between orchestrator
  machines for different game instances", requirement "(b) bundles move
  easily between machines", and "Reference-checking arrives with the
  pack-manager page (§8.1)". "Orchestrator machines" became "the
  machines that run the engine" (jargon row 66); "game instances" became
  "running copies of the engine" (jargon row 11). The line says "must
  move", the requirement on the finished tool, and keeps the roadmap's
  timing for the checking half: it "comes with the design tool's page
  for managing packs", the roadmap's pack-manager page in plain words
  (the site has no words-box term for it).
- The two sources name the requirements by what they ask (the final
  tool's requirement on uploading; on moving bundles), not by the
  roadmap's letters: visible letters are reserved for the owner's
  questions on the Decisions page (plan v2 section 2).

### The design-iteration loop

- The design-iteration loop, Games are playtested without code or
  settings files: "Making games playtestable without code or config
  files IS the point of the engine work (owner, 2026-08-29). The loop:
  edit pack values in the tool, apply, playtest, repeat." and the
  subsection's heading, "The design-iteration loop (a first-class
  platform property)". "Config files" became "a settings file" (the
  front page's words box wording). The heading's claim is carried in
  the second sentence as "the loop belongs to the platform itself, not
  to one game" (joined to the point-of-the-work sentence so the line
  keeps to two sentences; the groups have no text of their own).
- The design-iteration loop, The editor and live apply are built
  together: "Consequences: the mechanics editor is the loop's surface,
  and hot-apply (reload a pack without restarting the orchestrator;
  historical: E10) is what makes the loop fast instead of
  restart-per-change. The ratified floor: the editor and hot-apply are
  one deliverable (§8.6)." "Hot-apply" became "applying a change while
  the engine runs, between games" (jargon row 16); the title's short
  form is "live apply", which the line spells out. "The mechanics
  editor" became "the design tool's editor for the game's rules", so
  the tool keeps one name on the site. "Loop's surface" became "where
  the loop happens". "Ship" and "deliverable" are trade words and are
  gone from the title, the line and the source: "are built together",
  "built and delivered as one piece of work". "The ratified floor"
  became "the minimum the owner ratified" in the source, since jargon
  row 52 gives "the floor" another meaning (what stays with staff who
  log in). The historical id E10 and the registry number are dropped.

### Platform parity

- Platform parity, Phones and hardware scanners stay equal in ability:
  "Phone/web clients and the hardware scanners stay equal in engine
  capabilities: both can drive the same pack-declared interactions,
  including the interaction primitives (§7.1)." "Clients" became
  "devices" (jargon row 40); "interaction primitives" became "multi-tap
  player actions" (row 57); "engine capabilities" became "what the
  engine lets them do" (the front page's words box wording; row 20
  warns that "capability" collides with the planned capabilities).
  The source carries "(2026-08-29; reaffirms the 2026-07-18 'the
  hardware scanner is a first-class platform' ruling)".
- Platform parity, Equal in ability, not in screens: "Parity does NOT
  mean identical user interfaces: hardware differs (keyboard, screen
  size, personal vs shared), and a game's kit declares which device
  classes its players use and what part each plays." "User interfaces"
  became "screens"; "device classes" became "kinds of device". Split
  from the first parity rule because the roadmap's one paragraph holds
  four distinct statements and a line holds two sentences.
- Platform parity, Hardware scanner work waits on no game:
  "Hardware-scanner firmware work for this is committed platform
  engineering: it does not wait on any one game's design." "Firmware"
  became "the hardware scanner's code" (jargon row 69); "committed
  platform engineering" became "committed platform work" (the
  commitment is the owner's and the program's; the engine commits to
  nothing).
- Platform parity, Designers change pack data, never device code:
  "Everything a designer iterates on is pack data, never firmware. The
  standing principle: never design a mechanic whose iteration loop
  requires re-flashing a fleet of devices." "Firmware" became "the code
  built into a device"; "re-flashing a fleet of devices" became
  "reloading the code on a whole set of devices" (jargon row 69), which
  keeps the roadmap's scope: any device built from code, not only
  today's hardware scanner.

### The engine holds no opinion

- The engine holds no opinion, Game-design choices are pack and kit
  settings: "Game-design choices (identification, rosters, station
  scarcity, tracking policy, whether more engagement is better) are pack
  and kit parameters, never engine defaults (reaffirmed 2026-08-29)."
  "Identification" became "how players are identified"; "station
  scarcity" became "how scarce stations are"; "tracking policy" became
  "what is tracked"; "parameters" became "settings" (the site's word, as
  in "settings file"). The list is kept whole. The rule's title restates
  the rule rather than repeating the group's title above it, so the two
  titles differ (every title unique within its kind, plan v2 section 4).

5. No site text was found that contradicts a roadmap constraint outright.
