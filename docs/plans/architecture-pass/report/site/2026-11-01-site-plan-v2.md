# The owner's site, version 9: plan (the proactive frame)

This plan replaces `2026-11-01-site-plan.md` for everything it covers;
where it is silent, that plan's frame, design system and diagram
pipeline (its sections 2 and 5) still hold. It carries out the owner's
decisions of 2026-11-01: design top-down for the long term (the
proactive frame), the published protocol, the panel, the sequencing
names, and titles, not numbers. Their text is in
`docs/plans/architecture-pass/DECISIONS.md` (the decision record, by
topic). Published to the same artifact link:
https://claude.ai/artifact/RzL2FzCZ4eESSNMYwkkKPL.

**The owner's goal, in their words:** "design a long-term optimal
architecture for our engine that makes it intuitive and easy to
maintain, continue development on, and extend (proactive)".

**What the site must do now.** (1) Put the engine's requirements in
front: what each person counts on (the promises), how well it must
work (the quality targets), how it must grow (the extension cases) and
the standing rules, for the owner to confirm; the panel and the design
rounds design against them. (2) Show the design decisions ahead,
top-down, and the few already made. (3) Keep today's engine as
evidence: what works, and the sixteen design flaws as situations any
new design must handle. (4) Hold the owner's open questions and
everything decided, by title.

## 2. The frame, changed

- Navigation: The engine · Decisions (open count) · Requirements ·
  Design · Today's engine · Change log.
- The status line: "Architecture pass, design half: the requirements.
  Nothing in the design is decided unless a page says so. Updated
  <date>."
- Titles, not numbers. No visible promise, decision, question, flaw,
  finding, quality-target or extension-case id anywhere a reader sees,
  except on `changes.html`, where the old numbers are mapped to titles.
  Questions carry a letter for answering in the chat (A, B, C … on the
  Decisions page only). Flaws go by title alone; the anchor ids stay.
- Sequencing names only: the architecture pass's design half and build
  half; the steps by what they do (the requirements, the panel, the
  design rounds, the build plan, the hostile review, the final
  questions, the owner's sign-off); blocks by name; question rounds by
  date; test levels by name. No bare phase, step, block, round,
  decision or question number outside the change log.
- Wording: every term in `checks/jargon-table.md` takes its plain
  wording; its words-box additions join the words list, with the
  sequencing names.

## 3. The pages

### 3.1 `index.html`, The engine (prose under about 2,500 words)

1. **Header**: h1 "What the engine promises"; the lead
   (`front.lead`): the engine is being redesigned for the long term;
   what this site holds; where the owner is in it.
2. **Where the work stands** (`front.timeline`): one strip of three
   columns, done, now and next, using the sequencing names only, from
   the roadmap (its readiness ladder, later blocks and committed order)
   and the decision record. Now shows the design half's steps with the
   current one marked.
3. **What you can do now** (`front.now`): one or two lines, linking to
   Decisions.
4. **What the engine is for**: the promises data's `purpose`
   paragraphs.
5. **Who counts on the engine, and when** (`front.moments`, as in
   version 8, in the plain wordings).
6. **How well it must work** (`front.qualities`): one line per
   quality, linking to its group on Requirements.
7. **How it must grow** (`front.growth`): one line per kind of person
   who extends it, linking to the cases on Requirements.
8. **The engine in its world** (`front.worldMermaid`, `front.worldKey`):
   one picture of the people, devices, outside programs and files
   around the engine, with the published protocol drawn as the line
   between the engine's inside and everything outside. It shows what is
   required, not a design.
9. **Decided lately** (`front.decidedLately`): the latest decisions by
   title, each a `[[dec:]]` link.
10. **The words** (`front.words`): the site's words, the sequencing
    names, then the design words.

### 3.2 `decisions.html`, Decisions

1. **Header**: h1 "Decisions"; `decisions.intro` (answer in the chat
   by letter or title; each question stands alone).
2. **Open now**, in groups: confirming the requirements (the quality
   targets, with every number left for the owner to set listed in one
   place; the extension cases; the promises and their wording
   questions); standalone games; the game's record. Each question as in
   version 8 (status, what it decides, what we know, options and costs,
   recommendation), keyed by a letter and titled in plain words. The
   withdrawn test-rig question is not shown; the change log keeps it.
3. **Coming in the design rounds, no answer needed now**
   (`decisions.designRounds`): the top-level decisions in their order,
   each linking to its entry on Design; then the later choices.
4. **Decided, by topic** (`decisions.decided`): every standing decision
   from the decision record, by topic, each with its title, the date
   and one plain line; a link to the record's history in the change
   log.

### 3.3 `requirements.html`, Requirements

1. **Header**: h1 "Requirements"; `front.intros.requirements`.
2. **The promises**: the glance table (title, who, when, the quality
   targets and extension cases that serve it); then the ten entries as
   in version 8, with "Broken by" renamed "Where today's engine falls
   short", each link going to its situation on Today's engine; the
   planned capabilities by name, no numbers; the test cases called
   "test situations".
3. **How well it must work**: the quality targets grouped by quality;
   each with its title, situation, the number to set (if any), the
   promises it serves, its status and why it matters.
4. **How it must grow**: the extension cases grouped by who extends;
   each with its title, case, what it should need, what it must not
   need, when, the promises it serves, its status and why.
5. **The standing rules**: the roadmap's standing constraints (section
   2) in short plain lines, each with where it comes from.

### 3.4 `design.html`, Design

1. **Header**: h1 "Design"; `front.intros.design`: the design half's
   order (the requirements, the panel, the design rounds, the build
   plan, the hostile review, the final questions, sign-off); designs go
   top-down from the requirements; today's design flaws are test
   situations, not inputs.
2. **The decisions ahead** (`design.ahead`), in order: how the engine
   holds the game's state; the published protocol's form; where
   authority lives across devices, offline and standalone included; how
   games and venues extend the engine; how one engine runs as many
   separate copies; how today's engine moves to the new design. Each:
   what it decides, the requirements that drive it (markers), what is
   already settled around it, and what the panel and the design round
   will bring. No options are proposed here; the panel proposes them.
3. **Decided directions** (`design.decided`): the published protocol
   (what it is, how it is used, where it sits, what it is not, its
   cost, as explained to the owner on 2026-11-01); the test bench
   outside the engine, with "Proof" a quality; the test rig built at
   the start of the build half; the after-game boundary.
4. **Later choices** (`design.later`): the mid-level choices from
   version 8's step-3 list that still stand, in plain words.
5. **The earlier candidate** (folded): version 8's Parts page content,
   labelled "An earlier candidate, drawn bottom-up from today's engine.
   The panel compares it with its own proposals in its second round."
   Joins and proofs is marked as replaced by the published protocol and
   the test bench.

### 3.5 `today.html`, Today's engine

1. **Header**: h1 "Today's engine"; `front.intros.today`: this page is
   evidence; every design is checked against it; nothing here drives
   the design.
2. **What works today** (`works`): what each promise already has
   (version 8's "built today", by promise), and the pieces already well
   built.
3. **Sixteen situations any new design must handle**: the glance table
   (title, the promise it mainly touches and the others, findings, seen
   live or reproduced in a test) and the sixteen cards grouped by the
   promise each mainly touches, titles only; each card's second diagram
   folded and labelled "How the earlier candidate would remove it";
   then the proposed seventeenth (nothing declares a game standalone).
4. **Standalone today**: version 8's standalone section and the
   hardware-scanner table, folded.
5. **The evidence**: version 8's totals, set-aside table and code grid.

### 3.6 `changes.html`, Change log

Version 8's content, plus: version 9 (this site: the proactive frame,
the requirements, the titles); the jargon replaced, term by term; the
old decision and question numbers mapped to titles (from the decision
record's last table); the old sequencing words mapped to the new names.

## 5. The builder

`build_site.py` is restructured for the six pages above and keeps its
pipeline (diagrams drawn at build time, themed, checked). Its checks
grow: no visible id (`J1`…, `C1`…, `N1`, `QT1`…, `EX1`…, `X-08`); no
bare `phase N`, `step N`, `round N`, `Block N`, `decision N` or
`question N` outside `changes.html`; every promise, quality target,
extension case, flaw and question appears exactly once as an entry;
every title unique within its kind; the retired words only on
`changes.html`. The jargon table's rows that target the data files and
the builder's constants are applied there, so the data and every page
agree.
