# The owner's site: plan

The owner page "What the Engine Promises" (version 7, one scroll of
about 31,000 words) becomes five linked pages and a change log
(decision 29, 2026-11-01). This plan is the one source for the
writers, the builder and the checkers. Published to the same artifact
link as version 7: https://claude.ai/artifact/RzL2FzCZ4eESSNMYwkkKPL.

**What the site must do next.** (1) Close step 1: the owner confirms
the promises and the design flaws and answers the open questions. (2)
Be the reference step 3's design pages link back to; each rule page
joins this site later. (3) Show the whole engine in one read. (4) Feed
the roadmap rebuild. The structure follows those four uses: one page
per use, the change log for history.

## 1. Files

All under `docs/plans/architecture-pass/report/site/`:

| File | Page | Job |
|---|---|---|
| `index.html` | The engine | The front page: the whole engine in one read, and the way to every other page |
| `decisions.html` | Decisions | Everything open for the owner, each question with its evidence on the same screen |
| `promises.html` | Promises | The requirements: what each person counts on, when |
| `parts.html` | Parts | The candidate design: the frame step 3 starts from |
| `flaws.html` | Design flaws | What breaks the promises today, with the evidence |
| `changes.html` | Change log | Earlier wordings, what changed and why |

`index.html` is the artifact's main page: the publish step wraps it in
a document skeleton, so it has no doctype, html, head or body tags; it
starts with its `<title>What the Engine Promises</title>` and
`<meta name="claude:viewport-fit-sides" content="cover">`. The other
five are full documents: `<!doctype html>`, charset, a viewport meta
with `viewport-fit=cover`, their own title (`Decisions · What the
Engine Promises` and so on) and the skeleton's base reset copied in
(light `color-scheme`; `:root` padded top and bottom by the safe-area
insets; zero body margin; `img{max-width:100%}`;
`[hidden]{display:none!important}`).

Pages link to each other by relative paths (`promises.html#every-tap`);
the front page is `index.html`.

## 2. The frame every page shares

- The v7 design system, unchanged: its tokens, IBM Plex Sans,
  Condensed and Mono, the teal accent and the fault red, light and
  dark (`build_report.py` TEMPLATE `:root` blocks). One CSS string,
  inlined in every page.
- A navigation bar at the top: the site name (to `index.html`), then
  The engine, Decisions (with the count of open questions), Promises,
  Parts, Design flaws, Change log. The current page carries
  `aria-current="page"`. It wraps to two lines at phone width; it does
  not scroll sideways. Sticky, with `top: env(safe-area-inset-top,
  0px)` and a fixed `body::before` band painted in the bar's
  background.
- Under it, one status line: "Architecture pass, phase 1, step 1.
  The shape is a candidate; nothing is decided unless a page says so.
  Updated 2026-11-01."
- A footer naming the documents the page is built from.

## 3. The pages

Word counts are targets for the prose, not limits on tables and
diagrams.

### 3.1 `index.html`, The engine (about 2,000 to 2,800 words)

1. **Header.** h1 "What the engine promises"; the lead (`front.lead`).
   No eyebrow: the status line under the navigation (section 2) says
   where the page sits and when it was updated, on every page.
2. **Where things stand** (`front.whereYouAre`): open for the owner
   (with the link to Decisions), decided lately, what comes next.
3. **What the engine is for**: the promises data's `purpose`
   paragraphs, as they are.
4. **Who counts on the engine, and when** (`front.moments`): one
   column per moment (production stage, point in a show, release
   day), each with when it is, the people there, and the promises they
   count on, each a title link with one line on why. Columns stack at
   phone width. Then `front.standaloneLine`.
5. **How it fits together**: the hand-drawn whole-engine picture
   (`shape_svg()`) with its key, then `front.fitSummary`: three or
   four short passages linking into Parts and Promises.
6. **Around the engine** (`front.around`): what sits outside the
   engine and how each connects, the after-game article and emailer
   among them (decision 28). An optional diagram
   (`front.aroundMermaid`).
7. **How to use these pages** (`front.siteGuide`): one line per page.
8. **The words** (`front.words`): the page's own words first, then
   the design words step 3 uses.

### 3.2 `decisions.html`, Decisions (about 2,500 to 3,500 words)

1. **Header**: h1 "Decisions"; `decisions.intro` (how to answer: in
   the chat, by the question's letter or number).
2. **Open now**, in `decisions.groups` order: About the promises (the
   ten promises; A to G); About standalone games (22, 24, 25); About
   the game's record (19); About the next step (21). Each question is
   one block: the heading; a status tag (`open`, `applied` = "Applied,
   please confirm", `changed` = "Recommendation changed by the map");
   "What it decides"; "What we know" (short evidence lines with
   links); the options with what each costs, where the question has
   options; the recommendation or suggestion.
3. **Coming in step 3, no answer needed** (`decisions.step3`): the v7
   list, plus whether paper documents join the pack with "unlocked"
   recorded during play (decision 28, point 3). An item that has a row
   in the Parts page's alternatives links to it.
4. **Decided** (`decisions.decided`): decisions 20, 22, 26, 27, 28
   and 29, each with its date and one line.

### 3.3 `promises.html`, Promises (about 6,000 words)

1. **Header**: h1 "Promises"; `front.intros.promises`, including how
   to read a promise.
2. **The ten at a glance**: v7's "How each promise is kept" passage
   (`FIT_SUMMARY[3]` in `build_report.py`) as the table's lead-in, then
   one table. Each row: the title (link), who, when, the parts that
   keep it (links), the design flaws that break it (links, the main one
   first).
3. **How the promises depend on each other**: v7's overview diagram
   (the promises and the shared work) and the relations diagram (the
   promise-to-promise links), the second folded.
4. **The ten promises**, one entry each, in order: the title (h2);
   who and when; what they count on (`functional.need`); built today;
   the long-term goal (with `goalOpen`); kept by (part links); broken
   by (each flaw link with its one-line `how`, the main one first); in
   a standalone game; folded: what the planned capabilities need
   (`needs`) and the test cases (`tests`), whose summary line keeps
   v7's "Answers the pass's questions …" (`questions`). No "earlier
   wording" and no
   "what changed" note: both move to the change log. The flaw cards
   move to the Design flaws page; the entry links to them.

### 3.4 `parts.html`, Parts (about 6,000 to 7,000 words)

1. **Header**: h1 "Parts"; `front.intros.parts` (a candidate shape,
   not decided; how to read a part card).
2. **Qualities every part must have**: v7's qualities table, each with

3. **The parts in layers**: v7's layers figure; folded under it, "Which

4. **Five moments, part by part**: v7's reading key under the heading

5. **The seventeen parts**: v7's part cards (what it does, mainly

6. **Which part would run where**: v7's devices list and diagram.
7. **Where today's code tangles**: v7's tangles, folded.
8. **Where a real alternative exists**: v7's alternatives table.

### 3.5 `flaws.html`, Design flaws (about 9,000 to 11,000 words)

1. **Header**: h1 "Design flaws"; `front.intros.flaws` (how to read a
   card) and the diagram legend.
2. **The flaws at a glance**: one table. Each row: the flaw (link,
   with its id), the promise it mainly breaks and the others (links),
   the parts that would remove it (links), the number of findings,
   seen live.
3. **The cards**, grouped under the promise each mainly breaks (the
   group heading is that promise's title, linked): v7's card for each

4. **Standalone games**: v7's standalone section with the ESP32
   parity table folded.
5. **The evidence**: v7's totals, the set-aside table and "where each

### 3.6 `changes.html`, Change log (about 3,000 to 4,000 words)

1. **Header**: h1 "Change log"; `changes.intro`.
2. **Versions**, newest first (`changes.versions`): this site (version

   and what prompted it.
3. **The promises' earlier wording** (`changes.promises`): for each
   promise, its first wording as a job and what changed since.
4. **The cards** (`changes.cards`): what changed on each card.
5. **Recommendations the map changed** (`changes.recommendations`):
   E, F and G, before and after, and why.
6. **The words** (`changes.vocabulary`): what decision 27 replaced.

## 4. Overlays: what the writers produce and the builder reads

Three JSON files beside this plan. Every text field is plain text (no
HTML). Links are written as markers the builder resolves from the data,
so titles and names are never retyped:

| Marker | Renders as |
|---|---|
| `[[promise:J3]]` | the promise's title, linked to `promises.html#every-tap` |
| `[[promise:J3\|the night's record half]]` | that text, same link |
| `[[part:P5]]` | the part's name, linked to `parts.html#the-nights-record` |
| `[[flaw:C9]]` | the flaw's title, linked to `flaws.html#c9` |
| `[[flaw:C9\|C9]]` | that text, same link |
| `[[q:A]]`, `[[q:22]]`, `[[q:promises]]` | "question A" and so on, linked to `decisions.html#q-a` |
| `[[page:parts]]` | the page's name, linked to the page (`engine`, `decisions`, `promises`, `parts`, `flaws`, `changes`) |

Ids inside markers never show; a promise's id never appears in visible
text.

## 5. The builder

`report/build_site.py` (Python, run with `python3 -I`) writes the six
files from the data (`2026-10-31-cause-cards.json`,
`2026-10-31-engine-jobs.json`, `2026-10-31-standalone-lines.json`,
`2026-11-01-engine-shape.json`) and the three overlays. It reuses
`build_report.py`'s renderers where they fit (card, promise, part,
walkthroughs, the whole-engine picture, layers, parity, grid) and
removes from them what moved to the change log. `build_report.py` and
the v7 HTML stay untouched until the site ships.

### 5.1 Diagrams are drawn at build time

The extra pages may not get the viewer's built-in diagram drawing, so
every Mermaid diagram is drawn when the site is built and written into
the page as an inline SVG. No page loads Mermaid.

- `report/render_diagrams.js` (Node, the pre-installed Playwright):
  reads a JSON list of `{key, src}`, writes `{key: svg}`. Mermaid
  11.4.1 from `MERMAID_JS` if set (a local `mermaid.min.js`), else
  `https://cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/mermaid/11.4.1/mermaid.min.js`.
  It loads IBM Plex Sans 400, 500 and 600 from Google Fonts and waits
  for them before drawing, so labels are measured in the page's font.
  Each diagram gets a unique render id (`d-<page>-<n>`).
- `mermaid.initialize({startOnLoad: false, theme: 'base',
  themeVariables: …})` with these stand-in colours, and `fontFamily:
  '"IBM Plex Sans", system-ui, sans-serif'`:
  background and edgeLabelBackground and sequenceNumberColor
  `#fefef1`; primaryColor, mainBkg, actorBkg and labelBoxBkgColor
  `#fefef2`; secondaryColor `#fefef6`; tertiaryColor, clusterBkg and
  activationBkgColor `#fefef7`; noteBkgColor `#fefef8`; every text
  colour `#010103`; every border colour `#010104`; lineColor,
  actorLineColor and signalColor `#010105`; noteBorderColor `#010109`.
- The builder then swaps each stand-in in every SVG's `<style>` block
  and `style` attributes for a theme token: `--dia-bg` (`#fefef1` and
  its translucent and hsl derivatives), `--dia-node` (`#fefef2`,
  `#fefef6`), `--dia-cluster` (`#fefef7`), `--dia-note` (`#fefef8`),
  `--dia-text` (`#010103`), `--dia-border` (`#010104` and the darker
  derivatives Mermaid computes, such as `#010108` and `#01010e`),
  `--dia-line` (`#010105`, `#010109`). Colours written in the diagram
  sources map to tokens too: the fault red `#dc2626` to `--fault`, the
  owner teal `#0f766e` to `--accent`, the outside amber `#b45309` to
  `--warm`, the grey `#6b7280` to `--muted`. `report/diagram_theme.py`
  holds the whole map, `theme_svg()`, the tokens' light and dark
  values and the CSS; it was tested on all 41 diagrams of version 7. Sequence diagrams' fixed
  presentation attributes (`stroke="#666"`, `stroke="#999"`,
  `fill="#eaeaea"`, `stroke="#000000"`, `fill="#EDF2AE"`) are restyled
  by page CSS with attribute selectors, which outrank presentation
  attributes.
- The build fails, naming the diagram and the colour, if any colour
  literal is left unmapped. The `--dia-*` tokens get light and dark
  values in the shared `:root` blocks.
- `present()`'s sizing directives stay: flowcharts at natural size in a
  box that scrolls sideways; sequence diagrams compact, at least 720
  pixels wide, in a scrolling box.

### 5.2 Checks the build runs, failing loudly

- Every marker resolves; every `href` to `#x` or `page.html#x` finds
  that id on its page.
- No promise id (`J1` to `J10`) in visible text; "job" and
  "installer" only on `changes.html`, which records the words decision
  27 retired.
- Every promise, part and flaw appears on its own page exactly once as
  an entry; every question key in `decisions.json` once.
- Prints each page's size and its prose word count.

## 6. Rules for every writer and checker

- The owner's words (decision 27): a promise is what a person can
  count on at a given moment; parts keep promises; design flaws break
  them; a quality is something every part must have. Promises go by
  their titles, verbatim, never by number. The venue technician, never
  "installer". "Job" and "installer" appear only in the change log's
  history.
- Decision 28 holds everywhere: the night's-record half names the
  director and the players; what players receive after the show is the
  game's business; the night's record carries the game it ran and the
  texts the players saw; players' emails stay outside the engine; the
  Director Console stays a separate program that reads the record,
  with room to run inside the Review stage later; the two clocks are
  finding X-08 under C9.
- The shape is a candidate. Never write it as decided.
- Plain, short, active sentences; no metaphors; no em dashes
  (`docs/agents/process.md` sections 4 and 5).
- Every fact comes from the sources. A writer's log lists each new
  sentence's source; the page never cites files.
- Nothing from v7 is lost unless it was a duplicate, and then its one
  kept copy is named (`site/inventory.md`).
