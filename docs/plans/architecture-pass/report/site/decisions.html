A. Do the quality targets say how well the engine must work?

What it decides

Whether the quality targets on Requirements, grouped under twelve qualities, are the measure the panel and the design rounds design against, and which of the numbers they leave open you set now.

What we know
  • A quality is something every part of the engine must have so that a promise holds everywhere, not in one place. Each target tells a situation at a show, in rehearsal, at the desk or in development, so that it can be tested: what happens, then what must be true. The targets say what is needed, never how to build it.
  • There are forty-two targets under twelve qualities: staying up through a live show; every screen telling the truth; the designer's iteration speed; operating it without technical help; easy to understand and change, for people and for AI agents; easy to extend; testable without the live show; privacy and access; running well on the show machine and the venue network at the size of a show; standalone and offline; safe release and rollback; and more than one copy, kit or pack without rework.
  • Each target names the promises it serves, and every promise is served by at least four targets.
  • Most targets rest on a decision you have already made or a roadmap section you ratified. Three are proposed in full, A tablet reconnects to the same facts, Screens say when the engine goes silent and Every fact is decided in one place: they rest on the pass's questions and on the promises' test situations, which you have not ruled on one by one. Five are proposed in part: Set up the kit from the guide alone (that building the machine itself needs no terminal), A newcomer proves a first change alone (a page per piece of the engine, and the newcomer measure), Every request is checked against its pass (the automatic check for a committed secret), Sized for a two-hour show with headroom (the sizes and limits, and a preflight that refuses a start over a load limit) and Phone capacity is measured before the first phone (that the profile declares the number of phones and the preflight counts them).
  • Two targets wait on questions on this page: A standalone game is normal, never a fault on question M, and A standalone game leaves its own record on question L, question M and question N. The other open point of A standalone game is normal, never a fault, where the standalone fact is kept, belongs to the design rounds. A tablet reconnects to the same facts leaves what a disconnected tablet does with a scan to the design round on where authority lives across devices.
  • The panel gets the confirmed targets in its brief, beside the promises and the extension cases, and runs only after you confirm them (What the architects' panel sees, and when).
  • Fourteen targets leave a number for you to set. They follow, one per line.
  • A power cut loses nothing of the game: how long the engine may take from power back to a running show.
  • Crashed programs restart on their own: how many restarts the program watcher tries, and over how long, before it shows red; and to confirm ten seconds for the controls to come back, from the promise's test situation.
  • A tablet reconnects to the same facts: to confirm five seconds for a tablet to be back under its own station name, from the promise's test situation.
  • Screens say when the engine goes silent: how long after the engine goes silent a screen must say so (today, the scanners' polling interval).
  • A published change plays within minutes: the minutes from publish to playing, the promise's "within minutes".
  • Set up the kit from the guide alone: how long the venue technician may need from an unpacked kit to a go.
  • Power on straight into a running show: how long from power on to a running show.
  • A newcomer proves a first change alone: how long a newcomer may need to make and prove a first small change.
  • Game data stays on the kit: how long each kind of data is kept by default, per game.
  • Sized for a two-hour show with headroom: the kit size to design for, within a few dozen devices (tablets, scanners, screens, speakers); the time a scan is answered in; the time a live message takes to reach every tablet; the machine's limits for load, memory, disk and temperature; and to confirm half a second for a video to report started, from the promise's test situation.
  • Phone capacity is measured before the first phone: the number of player phones one kit carries.
  • A small pack with checked media files: the longest acceptable first sync for a hardware scanner (today 5 to 15 minutes for 30 to 40 MB).
  • Offline scans are kept and sent once: how many scans a device must be able to keep while offline.
  • Running copies of the engine stay separate: how many copies of the engine one machine must carry at once.
The options, and what each costs
  • Confirm the targets as the base and set the numbers now. Fourteen numbers to settle at once; a number set before anything is measured may need resetting once the build half's first version runs.
  • Confirm the targets and leave the numbers to the design rounds. The panel designs without them; a target without its number is harder to test, and each number comes back to you as a question in the round that uses it.

RecommendationConfirm the forty-two as the base. Set now the numbers you already know from running the show (how long a boot or a restart may take, the kit size, how long records are kept); for the rest, say that the panel may propose a number and you will confirm it in the design round that uses it. A target you do not want changes the brief the panel gets, so name it now.
