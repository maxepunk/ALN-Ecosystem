Design flaws

A design flaw is something in today's design that breaks a promise. There are sixteen, C1 to C16, and one proposed, N1, which question C asks about. Each has one promise it mainly breaks, and its card sits under that promise's heading; where a flaw also breaks other promises, those entries on the Promises page point back to the card. The table at the top gives every flaw at a glance, with the promises it breaks, the parts that would remove it, how many findings from the cause analysis sit under it (for N1, from the standalone pass) and whether it was seen live or reproduced in a test.

A card tells one moment at a show and why it happens. Where it was seen live or reproduced in a test, a note says how. In a standalone game says what the flaw does with no engine, today and in the candidate. Two diagrams follow. Today shows how the problem happens now, with the place it goes wrong drawn in red. Candidate, not decided shows how the candidate shape would remove it, with the one place that would own the fact drawn in teal. The card then names the parts that would remove it, each a link to the Parts page, and restates the reason in design terms: modules, interfaces, seams, locality. The problems behind it lists a few of its findings, from blue's shows, the maps of today's engine and the after-game survey, each with its id, and the code they live in. The card's head counts all its findings, names which of the architecture pass's nine questions it answers (these are not the questions on Decisions), marks a problem that was seen live or reproduced in a test, and names any fix made on blue that points to it, as a clue.

The standalone games section restates decision 22 and the two biggest gaps with no engine, with the hardware scanner's parity table folded under it. The evidence at the end gives the totals, the findings set aside with their reasons, and where each flaw lives in today's code. Nothing on the candidate side is decided; step 3 puts competing designs in front of you.

C12

Pack edits reach each copy of the pack differently

20 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 6, 5, 8

At a show A designer publishes a pack change and does not restart. The TV shows evidence by the new rules while the engine scores by the old. The file the engine hands out no longer matches the fingerprint in the engine's own pack list, so a scanner that checks rejects it. A tablet running a different pack is only noted in the engine's log; nothing tells the GM. The publish tells the running engine nothing.

Why it happens The engine freezes one pack at start, yet the files it hands out are whatever is on disk now, and it re-reads the token list from disk at every request. The TV, tablet and hardware scanner each load the pack at their own moment and keep it; the phone reads its own copy. No message says it changed.

In a standalone game Each device loads whatever pack its source holds; a mid-game reload can hand the tablet a newer pack; no stamp marks the game or any device. In the candidate each device shows its stamp, the Game record carries the game's, and Lifecycle refuses a swap mid-game. The other way, an engine of its own on the tablet, must do the same, kept equal by tests.

Removed by these parts of the candidate shapePack channelDesign studio

In design terms The Pack channel should own one adopted pack behind one interface: adopt, say which is current, fetch a file, announce a change. Each source is an adapter at its seam, and the Design studio reaches the engine's copy by message. Today the engine's frozen list and the live disk both cross the seam: no locality.

The problems behind it, and the code they live in
  • B-07 / D-04 After a pack edit with no restart, endpoints serve new bytes while the engine scores from the old pack; hash mismatches.
  • B-28 / D-38 Publishing a pack from the config tool tells the running engine nothing; the engine scores by the edit only after a restart.
  • B-20 / C-08 / D-18 A tablet or scanner on a different pack than the engine is only logged; nothing tells the device or GM.
  • D-03 Before a restart the TV shows evidence by the new rules while the engine scores by the old.
  • D-08 A new pack reaches a hardware scanner only after a reboot; tokens cannot be re-synced by command.
  • D-41 A password changed in the tool's env editor works at once on the tool, only after restart on the engine.

C1

Each tablet keeps its own list of claimed tokens

19 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 1, 8Clue from blue: fix W1

At a show A GM deletes a transaction. The engine frees the token, but every tablet that stays connected keeps refusing it as already scanned until someone reloads. A tablet that drops its connection during Reset All Scores comes back still holding the old claims, and refuses tokens the engine has cleared.

Why it happens The engine decides which tokens are claimed, but each tablet holds a copy that only grows: added on scan, merged on reconnect, removed only when a scan fails. The engine's own messages also skip facts that change as a side effect of something else, such as a team created by a scan.

In a standalone game One tablet holds the only list, but not every screen refreshes. Two standalone tablets are two separate games; one per game is undecided. In the candidate shape the tablet runs the engine's Game record and Lifecycle; or an engine of its own, kept equal by tests.

Removed by these parts of the candidate shapeGame recordLifecycle

In design terms The Game record should own which tokens are claimed, read from the transactions. Delivery's interface is the one seam between a claim and a screen; each tablet is a Screens adapter drawing it. Today the tablet updates its copy with one hand-written step per message; a message with no step leaves it stale: no locality.

The problems behind it, and the code they live in
  • A-05 / C-04 GM deletes a transaction; every connected tablet keeps refusing the token as already scanned until it reloads.
  • A-06 / C-30 A tablet that dropped its connection during Reset All Scores reconnects with its stale claims and refuses cleared tokens.
  • A-24 / C-29 After a reload a tablet knows only its own claims; a rescan of another station's token shows success, then rejection.
  • B-13 A delete recomputes every team's score but the message carries one team's row; latent today.
  • B-19 A scan for a team nobody added creates the team silently; no team message reaches any tablet.
  • A-25 / C-01 Session Details freezes Teams and GM Stations at the last full snapshot, because one message merges and one replaces.

C6

Reconnect snapshot and live update are built apart

11 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 9, 8

At a show A GM reloads a tablet mid-game. The History badge stays hidden until the next scan, and a rebuilt admin panel can show the clock as --:-- until the next change. The device list flips between every device ever seen and only those connected now, depending on which message came last.

Why it happens For each fact the engine has one piece of code that writes the catch-up snapshot a tablet gets on connect and another that writes the live message, and six places send the catch-up with hand-picked contents. The tablet applies each by a different rule, and a restore after a reload sends screens none of the signals they watch for.

In a standalone game There is no reconnect snapshot. Reading the saved game back at a reload tells no screen, so the History badge stays hidden. In the candidate the tablet's Lifecycle and Delivery send a restore like a change; or an engine of its own, kept equal by tests.

Removed by these parts of the candidate shapeDeliveryLifecycle

In design terms Delivery should own one builder per fact, serving the catch-up snapshot and the live message from each deciding part's changes; Lifecycle's restore sends the same, and Screens draws only what that interface defines. Today the snapshot builder is shallow: a caller hands it every module, and five tablet adapters reshape its output: no locality.

The problems behind it, and the code they live in
  • A-22 After a reload the History badge stays hidden until the next scan; the restore path fires no event.
  • A-30 / C-46 After a panel rebuild the first state push can be skipped and the clock shows --:--.
  • C-23 The device list alternates between everyone ever and connected only, depending on which message came last.
  • B-31 Six places send the full snapshot and disagree on which devices to include and which extra keys to add.
  • A-32 / C-44 Snapshot and live shapes differ for clock and cues; five adapters reshape them, and the clock adapter drops unnamed fields.
  • D-01 A TV reloaded during a video shows its last overlay state, because the snapshot's display status is never read.

C9

Tablets and phones decide facts the engine owns

19 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 1, 5

At a show A GM scans a token; the result screen says Transaction Complete with points the tablet worked out itself, before the engine has answered. The engine may refuse, and the correction arrives as a small pop-up message. In a standalone game the tablet is the only judge, and its rules have gaps: a scan into a paused game shows complete and records nothing. A phone says revisited for last week's token.

Why it happens The engine decides scoring, claims and whether a scan is allowed now, but the tablet carries its own copy of those rules, kept matching by hand, with a note in the code as the only reminder. The phone and the hardware scanner each choose a team on their own and decide on their own whether a token is new or already seen. Each device shows its own answer first.

In a standalone game The GM tablet judges claims, scores and scan permission from hand-kept rules; each player device decides whether a token is known. In the candidate shape the tablet hosts the engine's Game record, or an engine kept equal by tests; player devices share one rule as data.

Removed by these parts of the candidate shapeGame recordGame rulesScreens

In design terms The Game record should own every outcome a tap gets, running Game rules as one body the engine and the tablet both load. Answering's interface is the seam a tap crosses; Screens draws the reply. Today the tablet keeps its own copy of the rules, so a rule change lands in two places: no locality.

The problems behind it, and the code they live in
  • C-07 Result screen says Transaction Complete with tablet-computed points before the engine answers; the engine may refuse.
  • A-02 / C-09 Standalone: a scan into a paused game shows complete, records nothing and locks the token until a reload; an ended game still accepts.
  • A-34 A duplicate scan on the same tablet never names the team that claimed it; the local guard fires first.
  • D-09 / B-62 Every player scan carries a team no game declared; the engine discards it and only logs it.
  • D-10 A phone that played last week still says revisited for a token in tonight's game.
  • C-58 The tablet matches token ids loosely, the engine exactly; the tablet shows success and the engine refuses.
  • X-08 A sale carries the tablet's clock and a manual adjustment the engine's. The GM enters the first-burial bonus and the transfers as adjustments, so they printed nine hours away from the sales; ALN's Director Console shifts them back.

29 problems foundArchitecture pass question 5Clue from blue: fix W3, W5, W6Reproduced in a test

C7

A reply never says which request, final or stored

18 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 1, 8

At a show Player devices upload their queued scans while no game exists. The engine answers "processed", stores nothing, and both devices delete the scans. A GM's scan that can never succeed comes back as a vague error, so the tablet resends it at every reconnect with a pop-up message each time. A slow Reset System reports failure and the new game is skipped.

Why it happens The engine answers before, or without, saving the fact it reports; calls an unsaved batch processed; returns some failures as a named outcome and others as a vague error; and confirms a command by the command's name alone, so two quick presses cannot be told apart. Each device then decides for itself what a reply means.

In a standalone game Each device answers itself and ignores the answer: a refused scan shows Transaction Complete; a queued scan shows as recorded. In the candidate the tablet's Answering shows a refusal as refused; an engine of its own returns the same words, kept equal by tests. Each player device's record step returns the same words, from the one list.

Removed by these parts of the candidate shapeAnswering

In design terms Answering should own one interface, one request in and one reply out, web requests and the live connection its two adapters; the outcome words are one closed list every device reads as data; the Game record saves, then gives Answering the outcome. Today three reply shapes leave each device its own rule: no locality.

The problems behind it, and the code they live in
  • B-64 / C-32 Scan failures like no session or queue full come back as a generic error; the tablet replays them on every snapshot.
  • B-22 / D-13 / D-14 A batch arriving with no game is answered processed but stored nowhere; both player devices delete it.
  • B-21 / D-15 A batch retried after an engine restart is stored twice; processed batches are remembered in memory for an hour.
  • B-69 / C-35 A system reset slower than the tablet's five-second wait reports failure, and the follow-on create session is skipped.
  • B-65 / C-33 A command ack carries only action, success and message, so two presses of one action resolve on the first reply.
  • D-12 One failed batch is dropped by the web scanner on any 4xx but kept forever by the ESP32.

C2

Service health and service state travel on separate paths

6 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 2, 9Clue from blue: fix W4, W6Seen live

At a show If the lighting system or the music player is down when the engine starts, the lighting and music panels stay disabled until someone reloads the tablet. If the music player dies mid-show, the health row turns red alone while the music card keeps its playing look and the progress bar keeps moving. Why it happens Each venue program has two facts about it: its health, in one shared health list, and its state, in its own record. The engine sends the health row when health changes, and sends a program's state only for a hand-written list of that program's events. An outage is on no list.

Removed by these parts of the candidate shapeEquipmentDelivery

The problems behind it, and the code they live in

C4

At a show The GM ends the game and restarts the engine; the ended game comes back as the current one and player scans keep landing in it. When the game ends, a tablet that reconnects loses its history and the wall scoreboard goes blank. A once-only cue spent in game one stays spent in game two.

C5

C13

The engine does not know who is connected

24 problems foundArchitecture pass questions 7, 1

At a show A tablet loses Wi-Fi and reloads; the engine still holds the old connection under the same station name and refuses the new one for up to about 85 seconds. Two tablets started together pick the same station name from an open list. Standalone games

Your decision 22 adds a requirement: a whole game can run with no engine, the GM tablet and the player scanners each working alone, as a choice the game declares and never mixed. In a standalone game the GM tablet decides everything about the game (used tokens, scores, teams, the game's start, pause and end) from its own hand-kept copies of the engine's rules. Each player scanner decides only whether a token is known, and the phone also whether it has seen that token before.

Four design flaws cause no problem in a standalone game, four apply in part and eight in full. Each promise and each card says what changes. The two biggest gaps: nothing says that a game is standalone, and every device shows the missing engine as a failure ("Offline" on the phone all night, red "DISCONNECTED" on the ESP32, "VIDEO UNAVAILABLE, Rescan later" for a video that can never play).
