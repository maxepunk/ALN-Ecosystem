"Questions" are the nine of the program document's section 3: 1 who decides each fact (the authority); 2 does a service's state travel with its health; 3 how the engine reads the programs it drives; 4 can anything other than the live engine touch the live show; 5 does one question get one answer; 6 what each fact and copy does at a boundary; 7 does each component receive what its role needs; 8 how a copy may be updated and whether a screen can tell its age; 9 is the reconnect snapshot built like the live update. ## 1. The causes in short

Sixteen causes, ordered by how much each affects a show night, most first. The first four hold the four reproductions the program document names for the rig (section 4.1, step 2): the deleted token still refused (C1), the music panel behind live music (C2), the ended game returning after a restart (C4) and the reset that leaves music out (C5).

**C1. Every tablet keeps its own copy of each game fact and updates it by its own rule, on whichever events someone remembered to wire.** At a show a GM deletes a transaction and every tablet keeps refusing the token as "already scanned"; a reload does not help; a tablet that was offline during Reset All Scores comes back with the old claims. The design lets this happen because the backend decides a claim but each tablet holds a list of its own that only grows (union on reconnect, add on scan, unmark on one error), and the backend's own broadcasts leave out facts that changed by another path, such as another team's bonus moved by a delete or a team created by a scan. Behind it: blue's fix W1 (three more places that update the claimed list) and 20 findings, among them A-05 / C-04 (deleted token refused), A-06 / C-30 (reset while disconnected), B-13 (a delete sends one team's score). Questions 1 and 8.

**C2. A service's health and its own state are two facts on two delivery paths.** If Home Assistant or MPD is down when the engine starts, the lighting and music panels stay disabled all night; if MPD dies mid-show the progress bar keeps moving. The backend pushes only the health row when health changes and pushes a service's own state only on that service's events, so a panel learns of an outage only where a hand-written list happens to re-send its state. Behind it: blue's fixes W4 and W6 and 11 findings: A-11 (lighting), A-12 / B-33 (music), A-17 / B-56 (a volume change reaches no other tablet), B-34 (music's private connected flag). Questions 2 and 9.

**C3. The engine's picture of each outside program is a cache fed by a text or event stream, never re-read and carrying no age, and the video queue, the cue timelines and ducking trust it.** Blue's worst live incident, the video that stayed "stopped" for about 28 seconds, came from a parser that held each message until the next one arrived (A-68); a false "video finished" can fire two seconds in (A-61); music stays ducked after the queue is cleared (B-45); a cue with a 2-second video never completes (A-48). Behind it: blue's fixes W9 P0.1, W10 P1 and W10 F3, and 25 findings. Question 3, with 5 for the gates that read the cache.

**C4. No fact or copy declares what happens to it at a game boundary, so each boundary clears whatever its author remembered and clients infer boundaries from whatever arrives.** An ended game comes back after a restart and accepts player scans (B-61 / D-31); when the GM ends the game, every tablet that reconnects loses its history and the wall scoreboard goes blank (A-01 / C-39); a once-only cue stays spent into the next game (B-17 / C-38); a reload wipes a tablet's offline scans (C-05). Behind it: blue's fix W8 and 22 findings. Question 6.

**C5. Starting the engine and rebuilding it after Reset System are two hand-kept sequences that differ in what they include and in what order.** The reset leaves music out, so the music panel and music cues are dead until a restart (B-01); it wires the broadcasts before the persistence listeners, so a later Reset All Scores sends every tablet and the TV the old transaction list and cleared evidence comes back (A-53 / B-02 / C-37 / D-30); after a reset the cue engine hears video and clock events twice (B-03); the Display row reads red (B-53). Blue met the ordering fault while fixing W4; 9 findings. Question 6.

**C6. The snapshot a tablet gets on connect and the live message for the same fact are built by different code and applied by different rules.** After a reload the History badge stays hidden until the next scan (A-22); a rebuilt panel can skip its first push and show `--:--` (A-30 / C-46); the device list alternates between "everyone ever" and "connected only" depending on which message came last (C-23); six places send the snapshot with different arguments and keys (B-31). 13 findings. Questions 9 and 8.

**C7. A reply does not say which request it answers, whether it is final, or whether the fact was stored, so each client reads replies by its own rule.** A permanent failure comes back as a generic error and the tablet replays the scan on every snapshot (B-64 / C-32); a batch uploaded with no game is answered "processed" and both player devices delete scans that were never stored (B-22 / D-13); a batch retried after a restart is stored twice (B-21 / D-15); a slow reset reports failure and the new game is skipped (B-69 / C-35). 20 findings. Questions 1 and 8.

**C8. The engine assumes it owns the machine, and the tests assume they may use it.** The engine names its helper programs by fixed files in `/tmp` and kills by pattern over the whole machine; the test layers adopt the real display, audio and Bluetooth by default. On 2026-09-15 a unit test killed the live show's video player (A-75 / D-52); the harness ran a second VLC racing the engine's (A-49 / D-71); a killed engine left a full-screen player (A-64 / D-70); twenty-two suites opened a browser on the show TV (A-79 / D-54). Blue's fixes W9 P0.3, W10 P2, P3, P4 and C1 are all absent from this tree; 40 findings. Question 4, and the seam the preview engine needs.

**C9. The tablet and the player devices decide facts the engine is the authority for, from their own copy of the rules, and show their own verdict: first in networked mode, alone in standalone.** The result screen says "Transaction Complete" with points the tablet computed before the backend answers (C-07); a standalone scan into a paused game shows complete and records nothing (A-02 / C-09); a phone says "revisited" for last week's token (D-10); every player scan carries a team the game never declared (D-09 / B-62). 20 findings. Questions 1 and 5.

**C10. When a fact is unknown, failed, stale or partial, the screen shows a plausible normal value; the times and reasons that would say otherwise are never carried or never read.** A missing venue profile turns six services "not installed" and the panel reads calm green (B-72 / C-48); Check Now on a down service gives no sign anything happened (A-16); the TV keeps counting down after it loses the engine (D-22); a cue with half its commands skipped reports a clean finish (B-76 / C-47). 19 findings. Questions 8 and 2.

**C11. One question has several answerers inside one component, each with its own rule.** "Is a video playing" has seven answers and a paused video can be replaced by the next one (B-39); held cues and held videos live in two stores (B-40); the tablet has four session ids, five reachability flags and three copies of the status that gates scanning (C-26, C-28, C-25); the downloaded report's two totals disagree (A-08). Blue's fix W3 and parts of W5 and W6 land here; 26 findings. Question 5.

**C12. The pack and the other files the config tool edits are read at scattered moments, the engine serves disk bytes under a frozen inventory, and no message says the pack changed.** After a publish with no restart the TV shows evidence by the new rules while the engine scores by the old (D-03), the manifest hash no longer matches the file it names (B-07 / D-04), a tablet on a different pack is only logged (B-20 / C-08), and the tool's publish tells the running engine nothing (B-28 / D-38). 22 findings. Questions 6, 5 and 8; this is the ground hot-apply stands on.

**C13. The engine does not know who is connected: identity and role come from a word the client says or a default, so every socket gets everything and reads need no login.** Anyone on the kit network can fetch the whole game state (B-27 / D-27); the TV receives every transaction and all ten service domains and filters on its own side (D-25, D-26); two tablets can pick the same station name (B-70 / C-19); an ESP32 is recorded as a player scanner (B-71 / D-32). 23 findings. Questions 7 and 1.

**C14. Names, shapes and descriptions that join two places are kept by hand on each side, and nothing checks that the sides agree, so a mismatch fails silently.** Team Details reads a property the facade does not have and shows the wrong score (A-03, A-04 / C-06); Game Activity reads the lookup's wrapper instead of the token (A-07 / C-15); the video badge element was never in the page (A-28); the contract lists events nobody sends and misses ones the engine emits (B-06, B-50, B-67). Blue's fixes W2 and W5 land here; 35 findings. Question 5 is nearest; the check itself is outside the nine.

**C15. The deployed game is several separately versioned checkouts, a built folder, hand-copied media and hand-run steps, and nothing checks that they agree.** A rollback moves no submodule backwards (A-91), `pm2 restart` keeps serving the pre-rollback scanner build (A-92), and blue's token line and the development line were incompatible in both directions (A-90). 8 findings, all from blue's records. No question covers it: a gap to put to the owner.

**C16. A green test run does not show the real path works: fakes drift from the real classes, skips and vacuous checks pass silently, and coverage follows files rather than the path from authority to screen.** The unit tests stayed green while Team Details was wrong (A-51); a page-object selector never matched, so a delete step had been silently skipping (A-83); 62 end-to-end tests skipped on blue's last full run and group completion has no test on real content (A-82); not one screen image is checked anywhere (D-87). 21 findings. No question covers it: a gap to put to the owner.
